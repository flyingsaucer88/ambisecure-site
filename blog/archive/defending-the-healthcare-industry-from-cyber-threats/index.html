Defending the healthcare industry from cyber threats

Due to the volume of private patient data handled often, the healthcare sector is a favourite target for hackers.

Cyber threats

This is an earlier piece from the AmbiSecure engineering archive. Where the field has moved on, the link above points to current coverage of the same topic.

Healthcare security in 2026Connected medical devices are now in regulatory scope alongside records systems, extending the identity problem described here to the equipment itself. Cyber security threats overview covers the current position.

Due to the volume of private patient data handled often, the healthcare sector is a favourite target for hackers. It is more crucial than ever to make sure that the sector is ready to defend itself against cyber attacks as healthcare providers’ activities become increasingly digital. We’ll look at some of the most important measures healthcare organisations should take in this piece to protect both themselves and their patients against cyberattacks.

Set severe password constraints.

Weak passwords are a major source of healthcare security risks. Healthcare organisations should establish rigorous password rules requiring employees to develop complicated passwords and update them on a regular basis. Access to critical systems should require multi-factor authentication as well.

Employees are regularly trained.

Human error is still the most severe hazard to healthcare security. Workers should be regularly instructed on how to recognise and avoid potential security dangers such as phishing scams and ransomware attacks. It is also vital to foster a security culture in which employees are encouraged to report any suspicious behaviour.

Segmentation of a Network

To prevent cyberattacks from spreading throughout their systems, healthcare firms should segregate their networks. It is feasible to decrease the effect of an attack and the danger of data theft by isolating sensitive data and important systems from less secure sections of the network.

Routine Security Audits

Security assessments should be performed on a regular basis to detect possible vulnerabilities and verify that security measures are up to date. Healthcare businesses may take proactive actions to remedy flaws in their systems and procedures by discovering them before cybercriminals exploit them.

Encryption

Encrypting patient data is an important step towards preventing unwanted access. All sensitive data in healthcare companies should be encrypted both in transit and at rest. Even if attackers manage to access the network, this can greatly lower the danger of data theft.

Preparing for Disaster Recovery

Healthcare institutions must have a disaster recovery strategy in place in the case of a security compromise. This should include actions to isolate damaged systems, backup essential data, and promptly restore operations. Testing the disaster recovery plan on a regular basis may guarantee that it is successful in a real-world event.

Keep Software Updates

All software and systems in healthcare should be kept up to date with the most recent security patches and updates. Cybercriminals routinely target unpatched vulnerabilities in older software versions, making it vital to maintain security updates up to date.

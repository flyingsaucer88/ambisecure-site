Convenience, security and privacy: the three pillars of digital identity

The three pillars of digital identification are convenience, security, and privacy.

IdentityPrivacy

This is an earlier piece from the AmbiSecure engineering archive. Where the field has moved on, the link above points to current coverage of the same topic.

The three pillars of digital identification are convenience, security, and privacy. Our internet identities are equally as vital as our physical ones in today’s environment. We rely on them for everything from bank account access to social media login. Nevertheless, with so much of our personal information circulating in cyberspace, how can we be certain that our digital identities are safe?

The solution is in developing a digital identification system that strikes a balance between ease, security, and privacy. Here is when the three digital identity pillars come into play.

Convenience

Convenience is all about making it simple for people to use their digital identities. This includes creating user-friendly technologies that are easy to navigate and use. With simple and user-friendly digital identification systems, people should be able to authenticate themselves and access the services they demand quickly and easily.

Biometric authentication, such as face recognition or fingerprint scanning, and single sign-on (SSO) systems, which allow users to access various services with a single set of login credentials, are two examples of easy digital identification solutions.

Security

Security is perhaps the most important cornerstone of digital identity. With cybercrime on the rise, digital identity solutions must be designed to be secure. This includes using strong encryption to protect sensitive data and implementing multi-factor authentication to ensure that only authorized personnel have access to sensitive data.

Moreover, digital identity systems should be assessed and updated on a regular basis to ensure their long-term security. This includes monitoring for potential threats and vulnerabilities, as well as correcting any security problems detected.

Privacy

Finally, privacy is an essential component of digital identity. Consumers are increasingly concerned about the security of their personal data, and digital identity solutions must account for this. This means that any personal information acquired should be kept to a minimum and used only for the purposes for which it was collected.

Moreover, digital identification systems should be built with privacy in mind. This implies that data should be anonymised wherever feasible, and consumers should have access to their own data. They should, for example, be able to readily see and amend personal information kept about them.
